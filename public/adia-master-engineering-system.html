2.1 Explicit Runge-Kutta Family (ODE1, ODE2, ODE4, ODE8)

For an Initial Value Problem (IVP) defined as $\frac{dx}{dt} = f(t, x)$ with initial condition $x(t_0) = x_0$:

$$\begin{aligned} \text{\textbf{ODE1 (Forward Euler):}} \quad & x_{n+1} = x_n + h \cdot f(t_n, x_n) \\ \text{\textbf{ODE2 (Heun's Predictor-Corrector):}} \quad & k_1 = f(t_n, x_n) \\ & k_2 = f(t_n + h, \, x_n + h k_1) \\ & x_{n+1} = x_n + \frac{h}{2}(k_1 + k_2) \\ \text{\textbf{ODE4 (Classic 4th Order RK):}} \quad & k_1 = f(t_n, x_n) \\ & k_2 = f\left(t_n + \frac{h}{2}, \, x_n + \frac{h}{2} k_1\right) \\ & k_3 = f\left(t_n + \frac{h}{2}, \, x_n + \frac{h}{2} k_2\right) \\ & k_4 = f(t_n + h, \, x_n + h k_3) \\ & x_{n+1} = x_n + \frac{h}{6}(k_1 + 2k_2 + 2k_3 + k_4) \end{aligned}$$
2.2 Adaptive ODE45 (Dormand-Prince) & Error Control Math

Dormand-Prince uses an embedded pair of 5th-order ($x_{n+1}$) and 4th-order ($\hat{x}_{n+1}$) estimators with First-Same-As-Last (FSAL) property to minimize derivative evaluations:

$$\begin{aligned} k_i &= f\left(t_n + c_i h, \, x_n + h \sum_{j=1}^{i-1} a_{ij} k_j\right), \quad i = 1, \dots, 7 \\ x_{n+1} &= x_n + h \sum_{i=1}^{7} b_i k_i \quad \text{(5th-Order Solution)} \\ \hat{x}_{n+1} &= x_n + h \sum_{i=1}^{7} \hat{b}_i k_i \quad \text{(4th-Order Embedded Estimator)} \\ e_{n+1} &= x_{n+1} - \hat{x}_{n+1} = h \sum_{i=1}^{7} (b_i - \hat{b}_i) k_i \quad \text{(Local Truncation Error Vector)} \end{aligned}$$

The adaptive step-size adaptation rule scales step $h$ using tolerance scaling factor $\text{Scale}$:

$$\text{LTE}_{\text{norm}} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{N} \sum_{k=1}^{N} \left( \frac{e_{n+1, k}}{\text{AbsTol} + \text{RelTol} \cdot \max(|x_{n, k}|, |x_{n+1, k}|)} \right)^2}$$ $$h_{\text{new}} = h_{\text{curr}} \cdot \min\left( \text{facmax}, \, \max\left( \text{facmin}, \, \text{safety} \cdot \left( \frac{1}{\text{LTE}_{\text{norm}}} \right)^{\frac{1}{q+1}} \right) \right)$$
2.3 Implicit DAE Solvers (BDF-1/2) & Modified Nodal Analysis (MNA)

Physical multi-domain networks formulated via Modified Nodal Analysis (MNA) produce non-linear DAEs of index 1:

$$F(x, \dot{x}, t) = \mathbf{C} \dot{x}(t) + \mathbf{G} x(t) + f_{\text{nl}}(x(t)) - u(t) = \mathbf{0}$$

Using BDF-2 (2nd-Order Backward Differentiation Formula), derivatives are discretized as:

$$\dot{x}_{n+1} = \frac{3 x_{n+1} - 4 x_n + x_{n-1}}{2 h}$$ $$\text{Residual: } \mathbf{R}(x_{n+1}) = \mathbf{C} \left( \frac{3 x_{n+1} - 4 x_n + x_{n-1}}{2 h} \right) + \mathbf{G} x_{n+1} + f_{\text{nl}}(x_{n+1}) - u_{n+1} = \mathbf{0}$$

At each time step, the non-linear residual $\mathbf{R}(x) = \mathbf{0}$ is solved via Damped Newton-Raphson:

$$\mathbf{J}(x^{(k)}) = \frac{\partial \mathbf{R}}{\partial x} = \frac{3}{2h}\mathbf{C} + \mathbf{G} + \frac{\partial f_{\text{nl}}}{\partial x}(x^{(k)})$$ $$\mathbf{J}(x^{(k)}) \cdot \Delta x = -\mathbf{R}(x^{(k)}) \implies x^{(k+1)} = x^{(k)} + \alpha \Delta x \quad (\alpha \in (0, 1] \text{ via Line-Search})$$

3.1 Advanced 2-DoF PID Controller Formulation

Standard ISA/Parallel PID controller with setpoint weighting ($b, c$), 1st-order derivative filter pole $N$, and conditional integration anti-windup:

$$u(t) = K_p \left( b \cdot r(t) - y(t) \right) + K_i \int_0^t e(\tau) d\tau + K_d \frac{d}{dt} \left( c \cdot r(t) - y(t) \right) * \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{ \frac{N}{s + N} \right\}$$
Anti-Windup Back-Calculation Math
When actuator saturates ($u_{\text{raw}} \ne u_{\text{sat}}$), error feedback compensates integrator: $$\frac{dx_i}{dt} = e(t) + K_w \cdot (u_{\text{sat}} - u_{\text{raw}}), \quad K_w = \sqrt{\frac{K_i}{K_d}} \text{ or } \frac{1}{T_i}$$
3.2 Cascaded Multi-Loop Control & Bandwidth Rules

Cascaded architecture uses nested control loops to reject inner disturbances before they affect primary output:

$$\text{Bandwidth Separation Rule: } \omega_{\text{current}} \ge 5 \sim 10 \times \omega_{\text{velocity}} \ge 5 \sim 10 \times \omega_{\text{position}}$$

3.3 State-Space, Linear Quadratic Regulator (LQR) & Kalman Filter

Continuous Linear Time-Invariant (LTI) state-space representation with full-state feedback and observer:

$$\begin{aligned} \dot{x}(t) &= \mathbf{A} x(t) + \mathbf{B} u(t) + w(t), \quad y(t) = \mathbf{C} x(t) + \mathbf{D} u(t) + v(t) \\ \text{\textbf{LQR Gain:}} \quad & u(t) = -\mathbf{K} x(t), \quad \mathbf{K} = \mathbf{R}^{-1} \mathbf{B}^T \mathbf{P} \\ \text{\textbf{Riccati Eq:}} \quad & \mathbf{A}^T \mathbf{P} + \mathbf{P} \mathbf{A} - \mathbf{P} \mathbf{B} \mathbf{R}^{-1} \mathbf{B}^T \mathbf{P} + \mathbf{Q} = \mathbf{0} \\ \text{\textbf{Kalman Filter:}} \quad & \dot{\hat{x}} = \mathbf{A}\hat{x} + \mathbf{B}u + \mathbf{L}(y - \mathbf{C}\hat{x}) \end{aligned}$$
3.4 Field-Oriented Control (FOC Clarke & Park Transforms)

Decouples three-phase stator currents ($i_a, i_b, i_c$) into orthogonal torque ($i_q$) and flux ($i_d$) components:

$$\begin{aligned} \text{\textbf{Clarke:}} \quad & \begin{bmatrix} i_\alpha \\ i_\beta \end{bmatrix} = \frac{2}{3} \begin{bmatrix} 1 & -\frac{1}{2} & -\frac{1}{2} \\ 0 & \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} & -\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2} \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} i_a \\ i_b \\ i_c \end{bmatrix} \\ \text{\textbf{Park:}} \quad & \begin{bmatrix} i_d \\ i_q \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \cos\theta_e & \sin\theta_e \\ -\sin\theta_e & \cos\theta_e \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} i_\alpha \\ i_\beta \end{bmatrix} \\ \text{\textbf{Electromagnetic Torque:}} \quad & T_e = \frac{3}{2} p \left( \lambda_{pm} i_q + (L_d - L_q) i_d i_q \right) \end{aligned}$$
